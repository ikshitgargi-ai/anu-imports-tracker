# LCBO Trade Sample Refund (TSR) — Full Working Guide

**For:** Anu Imports agency team (agents of record, reps, bookkeeping)
**Covers:** what the TSR program is, exactly how much money comes back, how to buy a
sample so the claim survives, how to file, and how to reconcile the cheque.
**Last researched:** 19 August 2026. Program mechanics below come from the LCBO's
*Trade Sample Refund Program Policy* and the three LCB claim forms published on
[Doing Business with LCBO → New Supplier/Agent → Forms](https://www.doingbusinesswithlcbo.com/content/dbwl/en/basepage/home/new-supplier-agent/demo/Forms.html).
**Before you file the first claim of a new fiscal year, re-download the policy
document and confirm the refund rate and the per-product caps** — those are the two
numbers LCBO has historically adjusted, and this guide is only as fresh as its last
review (see [Keeping this guide current](#12-keeping-this-guide-current)).

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

You cannot get free product out of the LCBO to sell with. Every bottle you pour for a
product consultant, a store manager, a licensee buyer or a market-research tasting is
bought by you, at **full retail**, out of your own pocket. The Trade Sample Refund
program gives you a **partial refund — 31.5% of what you spent** — capped per month
and per product, paid by cheque roughly a month after you file. It is the LCBO handing
back its markup slice on product you burned promoting listings you already have. It is
**not** a rebate on sales, not a discount at the till, and not automatic: no form, no
money.

**The money math in one line:**
> You spend $100 retail on samples → you get about **$31.50** back → your true sample
> cost is about **$68.50** (plus you keep the deposit refund on the empties, separately,
> through ODRP).

---

## 2. What TSR is — and the three things it gets confused with

| Thing | What it actually is | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| **TSR (this guide)** | Partial refund of *your* retail purchases of your own listed products, used for promotion/market research | LCBO Retail Accounting, by cheque, after you claim |
| **Buyer / lab samples** | Bottles you ship to LCBO head office for a product call, quality assurance or a Vintages submission | You. Never refundable under TSR |
| **Returns & claims** | Damaged, spoiled or short-shipped stock, ODRP deposits, wholesale claim portal items | Separate LCBO processes entirely |
| **Licensee "sampling"** | Pouring for staff/guests at a bar or restaurant out of the licensee's own stock | You buy it from the licensee at menu price — an AGCO rule, not an LCBO refund |

TSR only ever reimburses product **you bought from the LCBO** — a retail store, a
Vintages store, Private Stock (regular or consignment) or the GTA Service Centre —
for a product **the LCBO itself ordered from the supplier on an LCBO purchase order**.

---

## 3. Who can claim

- **Registered agents of General Purchase, Vintages or Private Stock products**,
  including suppliers who act as their own agent. If you are the agent of record on
  the product, you are the claimant.
- **You must be in good standing with the AGCO.** A lapsed or suspended
  Manufacturer's Representative licence kills the claim, not just delays it.
- **Newly registered agents wait one month.** You are not eligible to submit or be
  paid until the month *following* the month you were registered. Plan the first
  tasting budget around that gap.
- The refund is issued to the **agent**, on the agent's payment terms with the LCBO —
  not to the supplier or the producer.

---

## 4. The three claim streams

You can claim in all three, but **each stream needs its own form and its own
submission**. Never mix products from two streams on one sheet.

| Stream | Form | What belongs here |
|---|---|---|
| **General Purchase** | **LCB 1897** — General Purchase Product Trade Sample Refund Claim | Everyday LCBO-listed products **plus all Vintages Essentials** |
| **Vintages** | **LCB 1898** — Vintages Product Trade Sample Refund Claim | Vintages release / non-Essential products |
| **Private Stock** | **LCB 1896** — Private Stock Product Trade Sample Refund Claim | Private Stock and consignment products |

**The Vintages Essentials trap.** Essentials are claimed on the **General Purchase**
form (LCB 1897), not the Vintages one — even though you bought them in a Vintages
section. The Essentials list is refreshed roughly semi-annually, so a product can move
between streams mid-year. Check the current Essentials list before you build the
month's claim; filing an Essential on LCB 1898 gets it bounced.

---

## 5. How much you actually get back

The refund rate across all three streams is **31.5% of properly documented trade
sample purchases**. What differs is the **cap**.

### General Purchase — capped at the agency level
```
Monthly limit  = (number of your listed General Purchase products) × $300
Refund payable = lesser of:  31.5% × documented sample purchases
                             monthly limit
```
The cap is a **pool**, not a per-product allowance. Ten listings gives you a
$3,000/month ceiling that you can spend against any mix of those ten SKUs — burn it
all on one brand if that is where the push is.

### Vintages — capped per product, three-way test
```
Per product, refund = least of:  $300
                                 31.5% × documented sample purchases
                                 2% of the bottles on the LCBO purchase order
                                   × 31.5% of the retail price
```
That third test is the one people miss: on a small release, your claimable sampling is
pegged to the size of the buy. A 100-case (1,200-bottle) release at $24.95 retail
allows 2% = 24 bottles → 24 × $24.95 × 31.5% ≈ **$188.60**, not $300. Sample harder
than that and the extra is on you.

### Private Stock — capped per product
```
Per product, refund = lesser of:  $300
                                  31.5% × documented sample purchases
```
Plus one hard procedural rule: see §6.

### Worked example — a typical month for us
| Activity | Retail spend | Stream | Refund |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 12 × $19.95 for an in-store RPR tasting blitz | $239.40 | General Purchase | $75.41 |
| 6 × $24.95 poured for licensee buyers | $149.70 | General Purchase | $47.16 |
| 4 × $32.00 Vintages release, PO was 900 bottles | $128.00 | Vintages | $40.32 (under all three caps) |
| **Total** | **$517.10** | | **$162.89** |

Roughly a third of the sampling budget comes back — but only for the lines where the
paperwork was done at the till.

## 6. Buying the sample so the claim survives

This is where nearly all lost money is lost. The refund is decided at the moment of
purchase, not at the moment of filing.

**In an LCBO or Vintages store (General Purchase / Vintages / Essentials):**
1. Tell the cashier **before** ringing through that these are **trade samples**.
2. Pay **full retail**. There is no trade discount; asking for one is not a thing.
3. Get the **original receipt stamped and signed by the Store Manager or their
   designate**. An unstamped receipt is not a document, it is a piece of paper.
4. Keep the receipt intact — **LCBO does not return original receipts**, so photograph
   or scan every one *before* it goes in the envelope. That scan is your only record if
   the claim is queried.
5. Buy samples on their own transaction. Do not mix them with anything personal or with
   product bought for another purpose.

**Private Stock / consignment:**
- Trade samples must be ordered on a **separate order from any other order of the same
  product**, and identified **in advance** as trade samples. Samples buried inside a
  normal order are not refundable, full stop — there is no fixing this after the fact.
- Keep the **original invoice stamped "Payment Received"** by the LCBO Private Stock
  Section, and the **original copies of both** the trade-sample order and the other
  purchase orders for that same product.

---

## 7. What does not qualify

- **Gift kits / gift packs** as designated by the LCBO.
- **Direct imports and unsolicited direct shipments** — anything the LCBO did not
  order from the supplier on an LCBO purchase order.
- Product you are not the registered agent for.
- Purchases with no stamped original receipt, or with the receipt lost.
- Private Stock samples not ordered separately and pre-identified (§6).
- Anything bought while your AGCO standing is not current.
- Personal consumption, staff gifts, and product resold in any form — beyond being
  ineligible, this is an AGCO problem, not a paperwork problem.

---

## 8. Filing: the monthly cycle

**The deadline is the whole game: a claim must reach LCBO no later than 30 days after
the end of the month in which the sample was purchased.** August purchases → file by
**30 September**. Miss it and the money is simply gone; there is no appeal for
lateness. (That cutoff covers General Purchase, Private Stock and Vintages Essentials.
Non-Essential Vintages release products follow the release cycle rather than that flat
cutoff — confirm the current wording in the policy before relying on extra time, and
in practice just file everything on the same monthly rhythm.)

**Our cadence:**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Day of purchase | Sample declared at till, receipt stamped + signed, receipt scanned, row added to the claim log (§10) |
| Rolling | Log which account/event each bottle was poured at, and the date — that is your defence if the claim is questioned |
| 1st–5th of the following month | Build the claim: one form per stream, totals cross-checked against the caps in §5 |
| By the 10th | Submit — well inside the 30-day wall, leaving room for a bounce-and-refile |
| ~30 days after submission | Cheque arrives; reconcile against the log |

**What goes in the envelope, per stream:**
- The completed claim form (LCB 1897 / 1898 / 1896) — product name, CSPC, store,
  date, quantity, unit retail, extended cost, per-product refund claimed.
- **Original** stamped receipts (retail) or stamped invoices + original POs (Private
  Stock).
- Your agent name and number exactly as the LCBO has it, and remittance details
  matching your LCBO payment terms.

**Where it goes.** The program is administered by **LCBO Retail Accounting**. The
mailing address on older copies of the TSR policy (55 Lake Shore Blvd. East, Dept.
#840) is **dead** — LCBO head office moved on 1 November 2021 and is now at
**100 Queens Quay East, Toronto, ON M5E 0C7**, with accounting departments identified
by department number on the address line. **Confirm the current department number and
whether Retail Accounting now takes TSR claims by email before you mail anything** —
ask your LCBO category contact, or start at
[Contact Us on Doing Business with LCBO](https://www.doingbusinesswithlcbo.com/content/dbwl/en/basepage/home/contact-us.html).
A claim mailed to the old address inside the 30-day window and returned to sender is a
claim you lose.

## 9. Getting paid, and reconciling

- **TSR refunds are processed within 30 days of LCBO receiving the claim**, issued as a
  cheque to the agent (or per your established LCBO payment terms).
- The payment is **per claim, per stream** — three streams filed for the same month can
  land as three separate payments on different days. Do not treat one arriving as all
  three arriving.
- Reconcile every payment line-by-line against your claim log. When a cheque is short,
  the usual cause is a **cap**, not an error: the General Purchase pool ran out, or a
  Vintages line hit the 2%-of-PO test. Re-run the §5 math before you query it.
- Anything still unpaid **45 days** after submission: chase it, with your scanned
  receipts in hand.
- **Bookkeeping:** book sample purchases to a promotional-samples expense account at
  full retail, and post the TSR cheque as a **recovery against that same account** —
  not as revenue. HST paid at retail follows your normal ITC treatment on the gross
  purchase. Confirm the treatment with our accountant; this is a description of the
  cash flow, not tax advice.

## 10. The claim log

One row per bottle purchased, filled at the till — not rebuilt from memory at
month-end. Template committed alongside this guide at
[`data/tsr_claim_log_template.csv`](data/tsr_claim_log_template.csv).

| Column | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| `purchase_date` | Sets the 30-day filing clock |
| `stream` | `GP` / `VINTAGES` / `PRIVATE_STOCK` — decides which form |
| `product` / `cspc` | Must match the LCBO listing exactly |
| `store_number` | LCBO store the sample came from |
| `bottles`, `unit_retail`, `extended_cost` | The claim arithmetic |
| `receipt_stamped` | Y/N — an N here is a bottle you have already lost the refund on |
| `receipt_scan_ref` | Filename of the scan; originals do not come back |
| `poured_at` / `poured_date` | Store, licensee or event the sample was used at |
| `rep` | Who bought and poured it |
| `claim_month` / `submitted_on` / `refund_expected` / `refund_received` | Reconciliation trail |

## 11. Pulling the log out of the tracker

The tracker already holds most of this, so the month-end build should be a query, not
an archaeology dig:

- **`tasting_blitz_log`** — `store_number`, `rep`, `bottles_used`, `tasted_at` is
  exactly the "where did the bottle go" evidence a TSR claim needs, per store visit.
  `tasting_blitz_photos` backs it up with a shelf/tasting photo.
- **`activities`** with a `sampled` outcome, plus `activity_sku_outcomes`, cover
  licensee and HORECA pours that never touch a blitz campaign.
- **`products`** carries `lcbo_sku` and `price`, which populate `cspc` and
  `unit_retail`.

What the tracker does **not** yet capture is the purchase side — receipt number,
whether it was stamped, the scan reference, and the claim/refund status. Until that
lands as a proper table, the CSV template is the system of record and must be filled
at the till.

## 12. Keeping this guide current

Verify these four things before each fiscal year's first claim, and after any LCBO
markup change:

1. **The 31.5% rate** — the single number the whole program turns on.
2. **The $300 caps** and the General Purchase multiplier.
3. **The form numbers** LCB 1896 / 1897 / 1898 and whether submission has moved online
   (LCBO has been migrating supplier processes into portals; a TSR portal would replace
   the mail-in flow in §8).
4. **The current Vintages Essentials list**, which decides §4's stream split.

Source of truth: the *Trade Sample Refund Program Policy* document and the three claim
forms on the [Doing Business with LCBO forms page](https://www.doingbusinesswithlcbo.com/content/dbwl/en/basepage/home/new-supplier-agent/demo/Forms.html).
LCBO also runs [Quarterly Trade Update webinars](https://www.doingbusinesswithlcbo.com/content/dbwl/en/basepage/home/updates/coming-in-2025---lcbo-quarterly-trade-update-webinars.html)
where program changes are announced — worth one person attending.

## 13. The AGCO side — what you may legally do with the sample

The refund is LCBO's; the rules on pouring are the AGCO's, under the *Liquor Licence
and Control Act, 2019*. TSR does not authorise anything the AGCO forbids.

- Sampling is done by or under an **AGCO Licence to Represent a Manufacturer**; a
  "sample" is a small serving given to promote a product, do market research, or
  educate about it.
- Anyone pouring needs **Smart Serve** certification.
- **At a licensed premises**, a licensed rep sampling to patrons must buy the product
  **from the licensee at menu price** — from the licensee's own stock, served by the
  licensee's staff, one-to-one conversation about the product. That purchase is not a
  TSR-claimable LCBO purchase.
- **Public promotional events** run under an **Industry Promotional Event** permit.
- If you are sampling or doing market research on a producer's behalf, carry a **letter
  from the manufacturer** confirming you act for them.
- Licensed convenience stores may **not** sample beer, wine, cider or RTDs to customers.
